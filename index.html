The boom playbook — what the winners looked like before they won

Reverse-engineered from five case studies. Booms are manufactured: a patient land-owner + a committed access project + a wealthy feeder metro + a hard supply cap. Hurricanes accelerate markets with intact demand (insurance-funded stock upgrades) and kill markets without it.

Nominal price CAGR during each market’s boom window

Entry timing dominates: the same Dare County that did 8%/yr from 2015 did 3%/yr from 2003 (bust included).

30A, Florida (1982 → )

The template

Seaside lots: $15K (1982) → $690K (2001) — ~22%/yr for two decades. St. Joe Co. converted 1M acres of timberland into WaterColor/Watersound; Mid-Bay Bridge (1993), the Truman Show moment (1998), and the ECP airport on land St. Joe donated (2010) each re-rated the corridor. ~40% of south Walton is protected land — the supply cap that keeps it expensive.

Outer Banks, NC (1984 → )

Access unlock

Corolla had ~15 residents behind a private gate until NC-12 was extended in 1984: homes went 422 → 2,750 by 2000. The boom ran on volume first, price later (Duck median $850K by 2024). Lesson: a committed road/bridge project is visible in DOT plans 5–10 years before it re-prices a coast.

Hilton Head, SC (1956 → )

The ur-case

Bridge (1956) + Charles Fraser’s 5,000-acre Sea Pines master plan. Oceanfront half-acres: ~$5K (early ’60s) → $200K (1973) — ~28%/yr. Fraser invented the covenant-controlled, amenity-led resort town every later boom copied.

Gulf Shores, AL (1979 / 2004 → )

Hurricane-rebuild flywheel

Frederic (1979) leveled the town → insurance built the first condo wall. Ivan (2004) → Turquoise Place ($1.5M avg units). Visitors 5.8M → 8.4M (2020–24). Still trades at ~half of Destin — the discount-neighbor convergence trade.

Port Aransas, TX (2007 / 2017 → )

The Texas proof

Cinnamon Shore (2007) imported the 30A template; Harvey (2017) damaged 85% of homes and financed a full stock upgrade. Median ~$300K (2012) → ~$670K (2026). Now the cautionary tail: 26 months of inventory — STR-supply booms mean-revert.

Failure modes

The setup without the boom

Grand Isle (direct hit every ~8 yrs + erosion + industry), Bolivar (Ike destroyed 62% of structures; unlimited lots), Matagorda (no master developer ever assembled it). Kill factors: storm frequency without wealth inflow, erosion, single fragile road, industrial adjacency, uninsurability, and no covenant-setting developer.

The 14 leading indicators (visible 5–10 years pre-boom)

1 · Master developer land assemblySt. Joe’s 1M acres; Fraser’s 5,000. Watch county deed records & REIT filings for >100-acre coastal positions.
2 · Committed access infrastructureBridges, road takeovers, airports — public in DOT/airport plans years before ribbon-cutting.
3 · Wealthy feeder metro within 3–6 hrsAtlanta→30A, DC→OBX, Houston→Port A. Track metro GDP growth + new direct flights.
4 · Hard supply cap on the coastlineState parks, refuges, reefs, height caps. % of beachfront in protected ownership.
5 · Lodging-tax receipts +10%/yr off a small baseCounty TDT data is public and leads price by years.
6 · Low STR friction + high gross yieldScreen AirDNA revenue ÷ median price > 8%; scan codes for caps and bans.
7 · Post-hurricane insurance-funded stock upgradeFrederic→condo wall; Harvey→new Port A; Michael→$1M Cape San Blas. Permit surges after storms.
8 · ≥50% price gap vs. nearest established compBooms flow downhill to the discount neighbor (Gulf Shores vs. Destin).
9 · A New Urbanist project breaking groundThe “Seaside signal”: Seaside ’81, Rosemary ’95, Cinnamon Shore ’07 — each preceded the steepest decade.
10 · First boutique hotel / chef restaurant / culture momentTruman Show 1998; Marriott’s first flag in Las Terrenas 2025.
11 · Permits doubling while population stays flatSecond-home demand leads census data (Corolla 5×’d homes 1984–95).
12 · Wide lot-to-home price spreadFat builder margins pull in spec capital.
13 · High-AGI in-migration to the countyIRS county migration files show the wealth arriving before prices do.
14 · Rising second-home/investor share of purchasesHMDA occupancy fields; falling homestead-exemption share.

Methodology & probability of appreciation

Process. Five parallel research agents ran in Aug 2026: (1) reverse-engineered the pre-boom profiles of 30A, OBX, Hilton Head, Gulf Shores, Port Aransas and Cape San Blas into the 14-indicator checklist above; (2–5) screened 55+ candidate towns across the Texas coast, the wider Gulf, the US Atlantic/Pacific, Mexico and the Caribbean against current prices (Redfin/Zillow/Movoto/broker MLS), STR economics (AirDNA/AirROI/Airbtics), STR regulation, insurance/erosion/hazard exposure, travel logistics from DFW/DAL, and named development catalysts. Full sourced dossiers are in the /research folder of this repo.

Scoring. Each town gets five 1–10 sub-scores. The composite is weighted for the dual mandate (vacation utility from Dallas + investment):

Composite = 25%·Access + 25%·Catalysts + 20%·Momentum + 15%·Value + 15%·RiskDrag   (scaled to 100; RiskDrag: 10 = lowest risk)

Appreciation probability. A separate appreciation sub-index drops Access (a house you can’t easily reach is still an investment) and re-weights toward what actually preceded historical booms:

Upside = 35%·Momentum + 30%·Catalysts + 20%·Value + 15%·RiskDrag → P(≥7%/yr nominal, 10 yrs): ≥72 → 55–70% · 62–71 → 40–55% · 52–61 → 25–40% · <52 → 10–25%

The bands are anchored to the case-study base rates: even textbook setups fail roughly half the time (Grand Isle, Bolivar and Matagorda all had “the setup”), and 7%/yr nominal ≈ a doubling in a decade — the OBX-2015/Port-A-2012 outcome, not the Seaside-1982 outcome. Seaside-class returns (20%+/yr) required buying from the master developer in year one and are not what this screen can promise.

What the model rewards. Named, funded, dated catalysts (Royal Caribbean’s $821M at Mahahual; Whitecap’s $800M at North Padre; St. Joe’s 165,000 entitled acres at Port St. Joe; SpaceX at SPI; Puerto Escondido’s 2024 highway + $200M airport) score far above vibes-based “hidden gem” press. What it penalizes: unlimited buildable supply, STR caps/bans (Oregon, Volusia, Corpus SFH), leasehold land (Navarre), agrarian/ejido title clouds (Puerto Escondido, La Ventana, Holbox), Level 3–4 security advisories (Jalisco/Sinaloa), and uninsurable erosion (Sargent, Dauphin Island, Cedar Key).

Key cross-cutting findings.

  • 2026 is an entry window, not a peak: nearly every US coastal market is mid-correction (DOM up, inventory up, prices flat-to-down) after the 2020–22 surge — buyer leverage is unusually high while the long-run drivers (feeder-metro wealth, supply caps) are intact.
  • The insurance stack is the new gatekeeper: budget 2–4% of home value per year all-in on US Gulf beachfront. It filters which towns can re-rate (MS wind pool < FL Citizens; FORTIFIED-standard Alabama < older Florida stock).
  • STR supply growth is compressing yields everywhere (+35–59%/yr in several screened markets). Supply-capped towns are the only ones that can’t build their way into a Port-Aransas-style 26-month glut.
  • Tulum is the cautionary benchmark: −48% condo correction, 30% occupancy — airports and trains don’t rescue oversupplied commodity product. Puerto Morelos (supply-capped, 25 min from CUN) beat it on every risk-adjusted measure.
  • International adds torque and title risk in equal measure: fee-simple English-law Belize and US-rails Puerto Rico are the low-friction routes; ejido/comunal Mexico demands parcel-level title forensics + title insurance; Honduras adds sovereign risk.
Caveats. Sub-scores are judgment calls on top of point-in-time data (Aug 2026); small-sample beach-town medians are noisy; several flagged figures ([GK]/[EST]/~est.~ in the dossiers) were not independently re-verified; probability bands are calibrated estimates, not measured frequencies. Verify insurance quotes, title, flood zone, STR ordinances, and HOA/condo documents on any specific property before acting. This is research, not investment advice.
