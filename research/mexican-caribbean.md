# Quintana Roo "Next Tulum" Deep Screen — DFW Buyer (Aug 2026)

**Cross-cutting facts (apply to all five):**
- **DFW–CUN**: ~20 nonstop/wk (~3/day), American + Frontier, ~3h00 (FlightConnections 2026). AA adds frequencies in peak season. CUN is the workhorse gateway for Puerto Morelos/Holbox; TQO for Tulum.
- **DFW–TQO (Tulum)**: American is the ONLY DFW nonstop — 5x/wk as of June 2026, E175-class regional jets, ~2h47. TQO handled 1,248,645 pax in 2025; DFW was its #3 int'l route (46,869 pax). Service continuing, but thin equipment + sub-daily = downgauge risk (ASUR 2025).
- **Chetumal (CTM)**: domestic-only — no US nonstops; ~423K pax 2025, down 2.4% YoY. MEX ~20x/wk. DFW→Bacalar/Mahahual = connect via MEX/CUN or fly CUN + 4–4.5h drive.
- **STR regulation (statewide)**: Quintana Roo Tourism Law reform published Aug 2025 — mandatory state registry (Retur-Q), fines to MX$100K for non-registration, 6% state lodging tax (ISH) collected via platforms, SATQ operating license, and each of the 11 municipalities can now authorize, limit, or prohibit vacation rentals (Yucatan Times 2025).
- **Foreign ownership**: entire coast is restricted zone → fideicomiso required: SRE permit + setup ~$1,500–3,000, annual $500–1,000, notary/legal 1–2% of price. US-style title insurance available but rarely used locally — worth the ~0.5–0.7% for any of these markets. **Ejido fraud is the #1 QRoo risk**: fraud reported in ~15–20% of foreign transactions statewide; Anade says 7 of 10 post-purchase consultations involve fraud (TheLatinvestor 2026; Tulum Times 2025).
- **Financing**: cash-dominant (est. 70–80% of foreign deals). Developer pre-construction: typically 30–50% down, staged payments. Cross-border USD lenders: MoXi (Global Mortgage) — 7–9% fixed USD, 35% min down, $250K–$2.5M, ≤65% LTV, 30-yr amortization; Mexican peso mortgages 9–12%.
- **Safety**: State Dept Level 2 for QRoo (mid-2026). Statewide homicides -56.8% in 2025 vs 2024 after federal deployment. But CJNG-linked cells run retail drug/extortion; extortion of businesses (cobro de piso) is the chronic under-reported issue for rental/hotel operators.
- **Tren Maya reality check**: Jan–Jun 2025 ridership 600,839 (+169% YoY) but only ~2,000 riders/day avg; fares cover ~12.6% of operating costs; profitability "up to 20 years" away. Real-estate-impact claims by promoters have not materialized — treat "Tren Maya station!" in listings as marketing, not a catalyst.
- **Sargassum**: 2025 set the all-time volume record (+40% over 2022 peak; ~120K tons collected statewide); 2026 tracking toward ~150K tons. Worst: Tulum, Playa, Cancun HZ. Reliably clearest: Holbox, Isla Mujeres, Costa Mujeres — and notably Mahahual per 2026 monitoring (historically episodic there). Hurricanes: all five in the hurricane belt; Holbox is a low sand island with severe surge exposure; Mahahual was flattened by Dean (Cat 5, 2007). Seismic: negligible.

## 1. Tulum — the post-boom benchmark

**What happened 2022–2026:** textbook speculative overbuild. Condo average prices fell 47.6% 2023→2025; residential lot averages fell from >$133K (2023) to ~$79K (2025); sales volume -40%; appreciation ~zero; open developer price war, mostly studio/1BR/2BR product (Tulum Times 2025).
**Rental economics:** 7,577 active listings; supply +98.5% YoY at peak; occupancy now 29–36% (AirDNA: 36%, -2.5pts YoY; AirROI Apr'25–Mar'26: 29.3% @ $193 ADR); revenue/listing -26.5% YoY to ~$12.6K/yr. Gross yields on 2021–23 purchase prices: ~3–5% and falling — many units cash-flow negative after HOA + management (25–30%) + 6% ISH.
**Title/enforcement:** 18 developments closed by authorities in six months to early 2026; active federal ejido litigation near Tulum National Park; lawyers report weekly calls from burned foreign buyers.
**Lessons:** (1) airports and trains don't rescue oversupplied product; (2) small-condo STR product is commodity — correction hit studios/1BRs hardest; (3) pre-construction "appreciation" was developer pricing, not market pricing; (4) ejido/permit diligence is existential; (5) sargassum + privatized beach access degraded the guest experience the ADRs were priced on.
**Bull:** buy 40–48% off peak in a destination with its own international airport and durable brand. **Bear:** years of inventory to absorb, occupancy ~30%, insecurity headlines, worst-in-state sargassum — dead money 3–5+ years.
**Scores:** DFW access 8 · Value 6 · Momentum 2 · Catalysts 3 · Risk drag 2 → **21/50**

## 2. Puerto Morelos

**Flights/drive:** best on the list — DFW–CUN nonstops, then 20–30 min drive.
**Prices:** ~$2,000/m² average condo; interior corridor MX$26.5K–40.8K/m², coastal strip MX$70K–91K/m² (2026); 1BR beach-side from ~$185K, 2BR from ~$280K, beachfront PH $600K+. Runs 15–25% below Playa del Carmen ($2,500–3,000/m²); steady single-digit annual appreciation, no spike or crash (Caribe Luxury Homes May 2026).
**Rental economics:** ~873 active listings; ADR $143–162, occupancy 32–35% (top-quartile claims 58%), revenue ~$11.6–12.1K/yr. Gross yield on a $220K 2BR at median revenue: ~5–5.5%. Fishing-village + reef-park demand is family/repeat-visitor — steadier, lower-beta.
**Supply/momentum:** only 17 vertical projects vs 201 Cancun / 184 Playa — genuinely constrained between reef national park and mangrove; anti-high-rise stance.
**Title:** mostly regularized private land in town/corridor; Ruta de los Cenotes inland strip has ejido parcels.
**Safety:** 2021 hotel-zone cartel shootout; since then heavy federal presence; profile similar to greater Cancun (Level 2).
**Sargassum:** meaningful — affected mainland stretch (better than Tulum, worse than Holbox); reef knocks some down.
**Bull:** the "boring compounder" — 25 min from a mega-airport DFW serves 3x daily, structurally capped supply, 15–25% discount to Playa. **Bear:** it never "pops" — low-key character caps ADR/appreciation; mainland sargassum + Cancun-metro crime bleed apply.
**Scores:** DFW access 9 · Value 7 · Momentum 6 · Catalysts 5 · Risk drag 7 → **34/50**

## 3. Mahahual / Costa Maya

**Flights/drive:** worst access: CUN + ~4h drive, or CTM (no US flights) + ~2h drive. Tren Maya doesn't reach Mahahual proper.
**Prices:** beachfront lots ~$1,200–1,800/m² near the malecón; lots from ~$50K; homes $100–400K.
**THE catalyst:** Royal Caribbean bought the Costa Maya cruise port + 34 surrounding properties for $292M, spending ~$821M total on "Perfect Day Mexico" (water park, beach clubs, hotels, retail; ~2027 opening); passenger projections ~1M → ~4M/yr within a decade; 1,000+ construction jobs 2025–27. Largest confirmed private catalyst in the state.
**Caveat:** cruise passengers are day-trippers inside a walled compound — CocoCay (Bahamas) generated almost no third-party rental demand. The case rests on worker housing + spillover overnight tourism + land appreciation, not STR income from cruisers.
**Rental economics:** thin; ADRs $60–100, modest occupancy outside ship days — a land-banking play, not cash-flow.
**Supply constraints:** effectively none — long empty coast both directions; abundant land means appreciation depends on demand outrunning near-infinite supply. Much raw coast is ejido-derived; stick to regularized lots with escritura.
**Hazards:** Cat-5 ground zero (Dean 2007); 2026 monitoring lists it among clearer beaches but history of heavy sargassum episodes. Remote healthcare/services.
**Bull:** $821M confirmed Royal Caribbean capex 2 years from opening, entry lots at $50K — clearest asymmetric land bet in QRoo. **Bear:** cruise enclaves don't create STR demand, unlimited land supply, 5–6h door-to-door from DFW, one Cat-5 resets the town.
**Scores:** DFW access 3 · Value 8 · Momentum 7 · Catalysts 9 · Risk drag 4 → **31/50**

## 4. Isla Holbox

**Flights/drive:** DFW–CUN, then ~2h15 drive to Chiquilá + 25–30 min ferry + golf cart (no cars). ~4h airport-to-door.
**Prices:** ~$1,200/m² indicative (Feb 2025); village lots and boutique parcels; very thin, illiquid market.
**Rental economics:** median occupancy ~45%, ADR ~MX$1,287 (~$75), typical revenue ~$11–12K/yr; top 10% command $324+/night — the money is in boutique-hotel product (8–15 keys, plausibly 60%+ occupancy at $150–300 ADR), not condos (Airbtics Feb'24–Jan'25).
**Supply constraints (the moat):** inside Yum Balam Natural Reserve (1994); construction moratorium (only 2 projects under construction); 12m height cap; sewage-plant obligations.
**Title — serious:** the "Holbox scam" — land amassed through questionable titles, some inside the protected zone; decades of legal ambiguity. Assume any parcel guilty until proven titled (Mongabay 2023).
**Infrastructure strain:** 7–10 tons trash/day, ~90K tons accumulated, failing septic (21 of 81 in one audit), blackouts, month-long water shortages triggering cancellations; hotel beds tripled ~2017–2020; ~150 hotels.
**Sargassum:** largely spared — among most reliably clear beaches in the state. Worst hurricane/surge exposure on the list (low sand island; Beulah 1967 split it).
**Bull:** hard legal supply cap + sargassum-free + top-decile ADRs = the only true scarcity asset in QRoo. **Bear:** may never get clean title; sewage/trash crisis can break the brand; a direct hit on a sand island is uninsurable-in-practice ruin.
**Scores:** DFW access 4 · Value 6 · Momentum 6 · Catalysts 5 · Risk drag 3 → **24/50**

## 5. Bacalar — lagoon town, NOT a beach destination

42-km freshwater lagoon ~40 min inland-south of the coast — no ocean beach, no sargassum; eco/wellness demand base.
**Flights/drive:** worst effective DFW access alongside Mahahual: CTM 30–40 min away but zero US flights; practical route CUN + ~4.5h drive or Tren Maya.
**Prices:** eco-lots from ~$30K, condos from ~$80K, lagoon-front homes $200–800K; brokers claim 10–15%/yr and pitch "Tulum 10 years ago"; counter-narrative: "the Tulum 2.0 playbook is a wealth destroyer in Bacalar" (environmental-legal exposure).
**Rental economics:** occupancy ~49%, ADR ~MX$1,338 (~$78), typical revenue ~$13K/yr; top 10% $260+/night. Bacalar's ~49% occupancy already beats Tulum's ~30% at 40% of the ADR. Boutique lagoon-front hotels (10–20 keys, $120–250 ADR) are the proven model.
**Environmental overhang (defining risk):** lagoon turned brown after Storm Cristóbal (2020); 2010–2020 north-basin nutrient enrichment +70%; ammonium, nitrates, E. coli detected; untreated wastewater discharges into the groundwater feeding it; the stromatolites are dying. The asset IS the water color; the water is degrading. Expect discharge/density regulation — bear case for developers, moat for early compliant owners.
**Title:** much surrounding land ejido/agrarian; lagoon-front federal-zone concessions complicate waterfront rights.
**Safety:** far quieter than the northern corridor.
**Bull:** cheapest waterfront entry in the state, occupancy superior to Tulum, Tren Maya station + free-zone Chetumal 40 min. **Bear:** DFW access terrible; lagoon measurably dying from the development you'd buy into.
**Scores:** DFW access 2 · Value 7 · Momentum 6 · Catalysts 4 · Risk drag 4 → **23/50**
